## Comparison dimensions

For each relevant system or paper, compare:

1. Who owns the goal?
2. Who designs the decomposition?
3. Who selects the tools?
4. Who decides what information is missing?
5. Can the AI acquire or build a missing capability?
6. Can the AI redesign its own working structure during the task?
7. Is success defined as task completion or observed movement in real-world outcome?
8. What human role remains?
9. Are authority and permissions separate from reasoning freedom?
10. Is validated operational learning retained across tasks?
11. Does the system explicitly study when human procedural guidance becomes harmful?

## Candidate novelty boundary to test

The potentially distinguishing combination is not any one mechanism in isolation. The hypothesis to investigate is the combined operating model:

> Human governs goal, values, reality, and authority; AI owns discovery of unknowns, method design, capability acquisition, execution, observation, and restructuring; completion is judged by observable goal progress rather than artifact completion.

This must be tested against prior work before being described as novel.

## Required output of the literature phase

The literature phase should eventually produce:

- a comparison matrix;
- closest prior systems;
- what GDSSA inherits from each lineage;
- what, if anything, remains distinct;
- terminology changes if an established term already covers the same concept;
- explicit claim boundaries.
